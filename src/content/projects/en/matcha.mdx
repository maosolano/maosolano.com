Matcha is a product by Lucho Molina for finding a rental in Bogotá: it saves your listings, asks what matters to you, and gives each option a *match* percentage. I redesigned its core flow, **add → prioritize → compare**, and built it as a mobile prototype.

**My role:** UX design with AI · **Period:** brief from July 24; prototype from September 12 to 18, 2026 · **Status:** prototype, not yet tested with users.

<Seccion id="problem" title="Problem" lead="Searching for a rental is already overwhelming. Comparing options should not add to it.">

Listings live in WhatsApp links, open tabs, and scattered notes. Matcha already organized that, but its core, the decision flow, carried three risks:

- **No real people yet.** Profiles and journeys were hypotheses nobody had tested.
- **A number can mislead.** When a piece of data is missing, the match drops, and "we don't know yet" reads as "bad apartment."
- **Prioritizing meant doing math.** Splitting 100 points distracts from what actually matters.

### Two decisions that changed the screen

<Pantallas
  items={[
    { src: "/casos/matcha/img/puntos.jpg", alt: "Previous version: Split 100 points, with a counter, bars, and plus and minus buttons", tag: "Before", caption: "Splitting 100 points: 2,092 px tall and math to do." },
    { src: "/casos/matcha/img/prioridades.jpg", alt: "New version: a word scale per criterion with live influence", tag: "After", caption: "One word per criterion: 1,364 px, 35% shorter, same calculation." },
  ]}
/>

- **Missing data.** An absent data point adds zero and the screen says so (“no data”) instead of hiding it. An incomplete option scores lower, and that is exactly what I want to observe in sessions.
- **Options first.** With their own apartments in front of them, people don't invent weights.

</Seccion>

The main product improved too: 26 of 55 links in the app did not look like links on mobile, and there is now a component with a visible underline, which is a system rule.

**What I still don't know.** The sessions with 6 to 8 people searching for a rental have not been run. I don't know whether a 43% caused by missing data reads as “bad apartment,” or whether people trust the match. That is the next step.
