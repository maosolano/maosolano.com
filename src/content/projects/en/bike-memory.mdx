Since 2020 I have been asking people to draw a bicycle from memory, without looking at one: friends, strangers on the street, bars, workshops, and galleries. Almost 400 people said yes. Almost none of the bikes would work in real life, and that is the fun of it: each drawing is a memory and a conversation. I turned that notebook into a public archive and a project anyone can join.

**My role:** author, designer, and AI builder · **Period:** June to October 2026 (most of the work between September 20 and October 1) · **Status:** in production.

<Seccion id="problem" title="Problem" lead="Almost 400 drawings nobody could see, and that were not only mine.">

Everything lived in notebooks, and showing it meant facing four problems:

- **Nobody could see them.** A single paper copy, with no backup.
- **Full-page scans.** Spiral binding, edges, and white space left the bike tiny.
- **No data.** Who, where, and when had to be reconstructed from memory.
- **No permissions.** They are other people's work, sometimes signed, shared without consent.

It was also an experiment: what can one person build with AI, with no development team, in a few weeks?

### From notebook to screen

Images go through a cropping script that removes the spiral and the edges and compresses them: from 72 MB to 12 MB. On 29 bikes the crop cut off the signature or the context, so I made a “sheet view” by hand.

Curation is private, so I don't show it: it is password-protected and lets me edit entries with “Save and next,” audit each drawing's anatomy (frame, chain, handlebars, and pedals), and review submissions in a moderation queue.

**Product decisions that protect the people who drew:**

- **A fixed wall.** Shuffling the bikes caused a loading error, and a fixed order keeps every bike in the same place.
- **Two separate permissions.** When submitting a drawing, I ask for permission to show it and, separately, to show the name. If I reject it, it is deleted.
- **Names without permission are hidden but searchable.** If you search for your name, you get your bike's number, never the name. This applies to 247 bikes.
- **No cookies, analytics, or ads.**

</Seccion>

- **Before asking for screens**, I wrote the rules Claude reads at the start of every session, such as “if there are two reasonable options, pick the simpler one.” That way each session started with the same memory.
- **Claude Design** produced the visual proposal; **Claude Code** wrote the code. Neither delivered the product on its own: I directed every screen and every decision.
- **AI proposes, I decide.** Passwords, security, and legal questions were mine to settle.
- **An experiment I put on hold:** I tried having Claude Haiku label the drawings by level of detail, and it marked 83% as “very detailed.” AI can suggest labels, but a person has to review them.

Today all 387 bikes are behind one link that also opens on a phone, and anyone can search for theirs or submit a new one.

**What is still unresolved.** Building was the fast part; the slow part is what AI cannot solve: whose drawings these are, what can be published, and who decides. There is a data inventory and 12 open questions for a lawyer. Until I have those answers, the shop and new uses are on hold, and I still need to ask each person who drew for their permissions separately.
