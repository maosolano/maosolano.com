VISTA is a [BitaGreen](https://bitagreen.io) platform for caring for urban trees, built for green-space managers, arborists, and urban planners. I designed and built a geospatial prototype with real satellite imagery for its first pilot, in Amsterdam, and a system of rules so Claude could implement it consistently.

**My role:** UX consultant with AI · **Period:** August 10 to 24, 2026 · **Client:** Vista / BitaGreen · **Status:** prototype with 2 of 3 modules working.

<Seccion id="problem" title="Problem" lead="The vision existed in documents, but not in a screen anyone could test.">

When I arrived, VISTA lived only in PDFs, sketches, and presentations. Between spotting a tree at risk and sending a crew there is a slow, manual review that requires GIS skills. Four obstacles stood between the vision and a screen:

- **Vision and execution were separate.** Ideas lost their intent on the way to the screen.
- **No design system.** Every screen started from scratch: slow, and unlike the others.
- **No decision log.** The same questions were debated twice.
- **A linear process.** It took weeks before anything could be tested.

- **What I defined:** the tokens, the design rules, the component sheets, and a review checklist Claude runs before handing anything over. Every decision is logged with its reason (41 in total).
- **What Claude did:** Claude Code (with Sonnet 5) built the screens and components; Opus 5 helped me with strategy, the handoff plan, and the proposal. Figma is connected to Claude through its MCP server.
- **What Storybook verifies:** it generates the component gallery from the code and compares the system's colors with the ones the app actually uses. It currently reports 29 in sync, 0 misaligned, and 1 unused. It does not measure whether the design is good, only whether the code matches the rules.

That check has already paid off: the rules said 16 px between cards and the code used 12 px. It is what justified adding Storybook and the automated checks.

It also left a documented workflow connecting design, code, and quality assurance, and two deliverables for the client: a handoff plan for their development team and a collaboration proposal.

**What I still don't know.** The goal of turning an alert into an assigned task in under 3 minutes has not been measured, because I have not tested the prototype with real arborists. Nor do I know whether this workflow works the same on other products: I have applied it only to VISTA. The client wants to continue, but it depends on them securing a budget.

</Seccion>
